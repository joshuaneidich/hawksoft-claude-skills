# HawkSoft Phone Log Standards

## Objective

Write phone logs that allow another employee to understand:

- Who communicated with the agency
- Why the person called
- What information was provided
- What action was taken
- What still needs to happen

## Recommended format

Use this format when appropriate:

> Inbound call from [CALLER NAME], [RELATIONSHIP]. Customer called regarding [REASON]. Customer stated/requested [IMPORTANT DETAILS]. [ACTION OR RESPONSE PROVIDED]. Next step: [NEXT ACTION, RESPONSIBLE PERSON, AND DATE IF KNOWN].

Do not force every note into identical wording. Preserve the facts and make the note easy to scan.

## Example: document confirmation

> Inbound call from John Smith, named insured. Customer called to confirm that we received the signed application. Confirmed receipt and advised that the application is pending review. No additional documents requested at this time.

## Example: ID card request

> Inbound call from Jane Smith, named insured. Customer requested updated automobile ID cards for the 2024 Toyota Camry. Confirmed the email address on file and advised that the ID cards would be sent by email. Follow-up assigned to the service team.

Suggested activity tag:

- ID Cards

## Example: address update

> Inbound call from Robert Jones, named insured. Customer advised that the mailing address changed to [ADDRESS]. Confirmed that the garaging and property addresses did not change. Mailing-address update requested effective immediately.

Suggested activity tag:

- Information Update

## Proposing activity tags

Tagging is part of writing the log, not a favor granted on request. Whichever surface
you are on — the desktop **Add Activity Tags** panel or the Agent Portal's **Activity
Tags** list — read the whole tag list first (the portal's groups are collapsed; expand
them), then match it against the facts you just wrote:

- Pick the one tag that fits best and apply it. Add a second only when the interaction
  genuinely covers two distinct things — most logs need one.
- Two candidates look close → take the more specific one rather than both, and rather
  than stopping to ask.
- Nothing fits → apply none. An untagged log beats a wrong tag.

Do not open a separate conversation about tags. The tag you chose is staged with the
log and goes to the user in the single review-before-saving approval, where they accept
it or change it.

Tag what happened, not what was asked for: when the list separates a request from an
outcome (`Cancel Request` vs `Cancellation`), the same distinction applies as in
[Do not claim completion prematurely](#do-not-claim-completion-prematurely).

The tag list is agency-configurable, so never invent a tag that is not on screen and
never report one as applied when it was not.

## Style requirements

Use:

- Complete sentences
- Neutral language
- Specific dates when relevant
- Clear distinctions between requests and completed actions
- The caller's name and relationship when known

Avoid:

- Vague entries such as "Called about policy"
- Opinions about the caller
- Unverified conclusions
- Internal sarcasm or emotional language
- Entire email or chat transcripts when a concise summary is sufficient
- Full protected or payment information

## Do not claim completion prematurely

Use accurate distinctions:

- "Customer requested"
- "Submitted to carrier"
- "Pending carrier confirmation"
- "Carrier confirmed"
- "Completed in HawkSoft"

Do not write that a policy change, cancellation, reinstatement, or coverage change is complete merely because the request was logged.
